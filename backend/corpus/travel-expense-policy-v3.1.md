# Travel and Expense Policy

**Aldermere Bank plc — Group Finance**
Version 3.1 · Effective 1 April 2026 · Supersedes version 3.0

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy sets out how Aldermere Bank employees incur, approve and reclaim expenses
arising from business travel and other business activity. It exists to ensure that
expenditure is necessary, proportionate, properly authorised and capable of being
evidenced to an auditor.

### 1.2 Who this applies to

This policy applies to all permanent employees, fixed-term employees, and contractors
engaged directly by Aldermere Bank. It applies in every jurisdiction in which the Bank
operates, subject to section 9.

### 1.3 Who this does not apply to

This policy does not apply to:

- Employees of third-party service providers, whose expenses are governed by the
  commercial terms of the relevant engagement. See the Outsourcing and Third-Party Risk
  Policy.
- Non-executive directors, whose expenses are governed by the Board Remuneration
  Framework.
- Relocation and assignment costs, which are governed by the Group Mobility Policy.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Necessity

An expense is reclaimable only where the underlying activity could not reasonably have
been conducted without incurring it. Attendance in person where a video conference would
have achieved the same outcome is not, of itself, a necessary expense.

### 2.2 Proportionality

Expenditure must be proportionate to the business benefit. The most economical option
that adequately meets the business need is the correct option.

### 2.3 Evidence

Every claim must be supported by an itemised receipt. A card statement is not a receipt.
Where a receipt has been lost, section 7.3 applies.

### 2.4 Transparency

No expense may be structured, split or described in a way that obscures its nature or
avoids an approval threshold. Splitting a single expense across multiple claims to remain
below an approval threshold is a disciplinary matter under section 8.

| **Claim period** | The calendar month in which the expense was incurred |

## 4. Approval

### 4.1 General principle

All expenses require approval. The level of approval required is determined by the value
of the claim and, in certain cases, by its category.

### 4.2 Approval thresholds

The following thresholds apply to a single claim:

| Claim value | Approval required |
|---|---|
| Below £75 | Automatic approval on submission. No manager action required |
| **£75 or more** | **Line-manager approval before reimbursement** |
| £500 or more | Line-manager approval and Department Head approval |
| £2,500 or more | Line-manager approval, Department Head approval and Group Finance approval |

A claim of exactly £75 requires line-manager approval. Thresholds are inclusive of the
stated value.

All values are stated in pounds sterling. Expenses incurred in another currency are
converted at the rate published by the Bank's treasury system on the date the expense was
incurred, and the converted value determines the applicable threshold.

### 4.3 Categories requiring approval regardless of value

The following require line-manager approval irrespective of value:

- Client entertainment of any kind
- Alcohol, where permitted under section 6.5
- Any expense incurred on a weekend or public holiday
- Any expense incurred in a jurisdiction the employee was not scheduled to visit

### 4.4 Approval must precede reimbursement

Approval is required before reimbursement, not before the expense is incurred. An
employee who incurs an expense that is subsequently not approved is personally liable for
it. Where an employee is uncertain whether an expense will be approved, they should seek
approval in advance in writing.

### 4.5 Self-approval

No employee may approve their own claim under any circumstances. Where an employee's line
manager is unavailable for more than five working days, the claim is escalated to the
line manager's manager.

## 5. Travel

### 5.1 Booking

All travel must be booked through the Bank's designated travel provider unless doing so
is demonstrably more expensive or the provider cannot service the route. Where travel is
booked outside the designated provider, the employee must record the reason in the claim.

### 5.2 Air travel

| Flight duration | Permitted class |
|---|---|
| Under 4 hours | Economy |
| 4 to 8 hours | Economy. Premium economy with Department Head approval |
| Over 8 hours | Premium economy. Business class with Department Head approval |

Business class is permitted without further approval where the employee is required to
work on the day of arrival and the flight arrives after 06:00 local time.

First class air travel is not permitted under any circumstances.

### 5.3 Rail travel

Standard class for journeys under two hours. First class is permitted for journeys of two
hours or more where an advance fare is purchased.

### 5.4 Ground transport

Public transport should be used where it is practical. Taxis and ride-hailing services
are permitted where public transport is not available, where the employee is carrying
equipment, where travel is between 22:00 and 06:00, or where personal safety is a
reasonable concern.

Private hire cars on an hourly or daily basis require Department Head approval.

### 5.5 Personal vehicle

Mileage is reimbursed at the rate published annually by Group Finance. The rate for the
2026–27 financial year is 45 pence per mile for the first 10,000 miles in a financial
year and 25 pence per mile thereafter.

Mileage is claimable only for the distance in excess of the employee's normal commute.

## 6. Accommodation and subsistence

### 6.1 Accommodation

Accommodation must be booked at the standard rate available through the designated
provider. Where an employee books accommodation outside the provider, the claim must not
exceed the rate that would have been available through it.

### 6.2 Accommodation caps

| Location | Cap per night |
|---|---|
| London | £250 |
| Other UK cities | £180 |
| Singapore, Hong Kong, New York, Tokyo | £300 |
| All other locations | £200 |

Caps are inclusive of taxes and service charges and exclusive of breakfast where
breakfast is charged separately.

Exceeding a cap requires Department Head approval and a recorded reason. Conference
accommodation booked as part of a delegate package is exempt from the caps.

### 6.3 Subsistence

Meals are reimbursable on days involving business travel, subject to the following daily
limits:

| | Limit |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | £15 |
| Lunch | £20 |
| Evening meal | £40 |

Daily limits are per meal and are not transferable between meals. An employee who does
not claim breakfast may not add the unclaimed amount to their evening meal.

### 6.4 Meals not involving travel

Meals taken at or near the normal place of work are not reimbursable, except where the
employee is required to work beyond 20:00, in which case an evening meal up to £25 is
reimbursable.

### 6.5 Alcohol

Alcohol is reimbursable only as part of a meal, only where a client is present, and only
up to £30 per person. Alcohol claimed without a client present is not reimbursable in any
circumstances.

## 7. Making a claim

### 7.1 Deadline

Claims must be submitted within 60 days of the date the expense was incurred. Claims
submitted after 60 days require Department Head approval. Claims submitted after 120 days
will not be reimbursed.

### 7.2 Required information

Every claim must record the date, the amount, the currency, the category, the business
purpose, and — where the expense relates to entertainment — the names and organisations of
all attendees.

### 7.3 Lost receipts

Where an itemised receipt has been lost, the employee may submit a declaration recording
the date, amount, supplier and business purpose, and confirming the expense was incurred.
A declaration may be used no more than three times in any rolling twelve-month period and
requires line-manager approval regardless of the claim value.

### 7.4 Reimbursement

Approved claims are reimbursed with the next payroll run following approval.

---

## 8. Breaches

A claim that is knowingly false, inflated, or structured to avoid an approval threshold is
a disciplinary matter and may constitute fraud. Group Finance will refer suspected cases
to Employee Relations and, where appropriate, to Financial Crime.

Repeated failure to provide required evidence, without any suggestion of dishonesty, will
be addressed with the employee's line manager in the first instance.

---

## 9. Local variation

Where local law or a local regulator imposes a requirement inconsistent with this policy,
the local requirement prevails. The local entity's Finance lead must record the variation
and notify Group Finance within 30 days.

Local variations in force are listed in the Group Finance policy register.
