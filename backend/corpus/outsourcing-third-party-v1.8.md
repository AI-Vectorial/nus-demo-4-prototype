# Outsourcing and Third-Party Risk Policy

**Aldermere Bank plc — Group Operational Risk**
Version 1.8 · Effective 1 March 2026 · Supersedes version 1.7

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy governs how Aldermere Bank engages third parties to perform activities on its
behalf, and how the risks arising from those arrangements are identified, approved and
managed.

### 1.2 Scope

This policy applies to any arrangement under which a third party performs, on a
continuing basis, an activity that the Bank would otherwise perform itself. It applies
regardless of contract value and regardless of whether the third party is an affiliate.

### 1.3 What this policy does not cover

- One-off purchases of goods, which are governed by Group Procurement standards
- Professional advice engaged for a specific matter
- Market data and information services, unless the Bank depends on them operationally

---

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Accountability cannot be outsourced

The Bank remains fully accountable to its clients and its regulators for any activity
performed on its behalf. A failure by a provider is a failure by the Bank.

### 2.2 Proportionate governance

The depth of due diligence, approval and oversight is proportionate to the criticality of
the arrangement, assessed under section 3.

### 2.3 Exit must be possible

No arrangement may be entered into unless the Bank can exit it without unacceptable
disruption. Exit planning is a precondition of approval, not a response to failure.

### 2.4 Data leaves the Bank only under control

Where an arrangement involves the Bank's records or personal data leaving the Bank's own
systems, the controls in section 6 apply without exception.

---

## 3. Criticality assessment

### 3.1 Categories

| Category | Test |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Failure would materially disrupt a core banking service, breach a regulatory obligation, or cause material client detriment |
| **Important** | Failure would disrupt an internal function materially but not a client-facing service |
| **Standard** | Failure would be inconvenient and recoverable within normal operations |

### 3.2 Arrangements that are always Critical

- Any arrangement involving the processing, storage or transmission of client identifying
  data outside the Bank's own systems
- Any arrangement on which a payment, settlement or custody service depends
- Any arrangement performing customer due diligence on the Bank's behalf
- Any arrangement whose failure would prevent the Bank from meeting a regulatory
  reporting deadline

### 3.3 Assessment and challenge

Criticality is assessed by the business sponsor and independently challenged by Group
Operational Risk. Where the two do not agree, the higher category applies.

---

## 4. Approval

### 4.1 Approval authority

| Category | Approval required |
|---|---|
| Standard | Business sponsor at Department Head level |
| Important | Department Head and Group Operational Risk |
| **Critical** | **Department Head, Group Operational Risk, Group Legal and the Outsourcing Committee** |

### 4.2 What must be in place before approval

- A completed due diligence assessment of the provider
- A criticality assessment, independently challenged
- A written exit plan meeting section 7
- Confirmation that the contractual terms in section 5 are present
- For Critical arrangements, confirmation that the relevant regulator has been notified
  where notification is required

### 4.3 Regulatory notification

Critical arrangements may require notification to, or non-objection from, a regulator
before commencement. Group Legal determines what is required in each jurisdiction and
records it in the outsourcing register.

---

## 5. Contractual requirements

### 5.1 Required terms

Every arrangement in scope must include:

| | |
|---|---|
| Scope | A precise description of the activity performed |
| Service levels | Measurable, with consequences for failure |
| Audit rights | The Bank's right to audit, and the regulator's right of access |
| Sub-contracting | No sub-contracting of a Critical activity without the Bank's prior written consent |
| Data protection | Obligations at least equivalent to the Bank's own |
| Business continuity | The provider's obligations, tested at a stated frequency |
| Termination | The Bank's right to terminate, including for regulatory reasons |
| **Records and data on exit** | **Section 6.4 applies** |

### 5.2 Terms that may not be accepted

- A limitation of liability that excludes regulatory fines arising from the provider's
  failure
- Any restriction on the Bank's or a regulator's right of access
- Automatic renewal without a review point
- A unilateral right for the provider to vary the service materially

## 6. Data held by providers

### 6.1 The Bank's records remain the Bank's records

Records created or held by a provider in performing the arrangement are records of the
Bank. They are subject to the Bank's classification and handling requirements as set out
in the Records Retention and Data Handling Policy.

### 6.2 Location

The location of data storage must be recorded before the arrangement commences. A change
of location requires the Bank's prior written consent and, where it crosses a border, an
approved transfer mechanism.

### 6.3 Access and segregation

Client identifying data held by a provider must be logically segregated from that
provider's other clients' data, and access must be restricted to named individuals.

### 6.4 Return and destruction on exit

On termination or expiry of an arrangement, the provider must return all of the Bank's
records in a usable format and then **destroy all copies within five years of the date of
termination**, certifying destruction in writing.

The Bank's contract templates specify a five-year maximum retention by the provider after
exit so that data does not persist indefinitely in systems the Bank no longer controls.

> **Note for business sponsors.** The Records Retention and Data Handling Policy
> specifies retention periods that may be longer than the period stated in this section.
> Where a provider is required to destroy records before the Bank's own retention period
> has expired, the Bank must ensure it holds its own copy. **Where the two requirements
> cannot both be satisfied, escalate to Group Legal and Group Operational Risk jointly.
> Do not resolve the conflict locally.**

## 7. Exit planning

### 7.1 Requirement

Every Critical and Important arrangement has a written exit plan, approved before
commencement and reviewed annually.

### 7.2 Content

An exit plan states how the activity would be brought back in-house or moved to an
alternative provider, over what period, at what cost, and what data and documentation
would be required. It identifies the point at which exit becomes impracticable and what
mitigates that.

### 7.3 Testing

Exit plans for Critical arrangements are tested at least every two years. Testing may be
a documented walkthrough; it need not be an actual migration.

---

## 8. Ongoing oversight

### 8.1 Frequency

| Category | Review |
|---|---|
| Critical | Quarterly performance review, annual full reassessment |
| Important | Semi-annual performance review, biennial reassessment |
| Standard | Annual performance review |

### 8.2 The register

Group Operational Risk maintains the outsourcing register recording every arrangement in
scope, its category, its approval, its exit plan status and its next review date. The
register is reported to the Outsourcing Committee quarterly.

### 8.3 Concentration

Group Operational Risk assesses concentration risk annually — where multiple Critical
arrangements depend on a single provider, or on providers sharing a single underlying
infrastructure.

---

## 9. Incidents

An incident at a provider affecting the Bank's services, data or regulatory obligations is
reported to Group Operational Risk within 24 hours of the Bank becoming aware, and
managed under the Operational Incident Policy.

The provider's obligation to notify the Bank within a defined period is a required
contractual term under section 5.1.
