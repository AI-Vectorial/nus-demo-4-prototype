## What we are building

A staff member asks a policy question in plain language and gets an answer grounded in Aldermere's approved documents, with its sources visible.
When the corpus cannot support an answer, the assistant refuses; staff can continue a conversation, browse cited policy and return to prior conversations.

## Who it is for, and the moment they need it

Operations and front-office staff need an answer while handling a client, settlement or onboarding task.
The policy question is an interruption to their work, not the work itself.

## Why it matters

Today staff search several places or interrupt colleagues, and may act on an outdated or unsupported answer.
A cited, current source makes the answer checkable; a refusal is safer than an invented policy.

## What we are NOT building

*Different from "Out of scope" below. These are things we would not build even
with another day — they are someone else's product. Out of scope is what we cut
for time.*

- A general-purpose assistant, web search or answers from model knowledge instead of the approved corpus.
- Policy authoring, exception decisions or customer-facing use; those remain outside this staff tool.

## Acceptance criteria

*Every line is pass or fail. No "properly", "correctly", "well". Someone who
has not read the PRD should be able to check each one.*

- [ ] Running `python -m app.ingest` on an empty collection indexes the full corpus; a second run leaves passage counts and IDs unchanged, passages retain the required fields including superseded status, embedding calls are batched, and `--force` recreates and re-embeds the collection. *(REQ-I1–REQ-I7)*
- [ ] All ten answerable evaluation questions return their expected section in the top three against the live index. *(REQ-R1, REQ-R5)*
- [ ] The ten answerable questions are not refused, all three out-of-scope questions return explicit refusals, the evaluation records their scores, and refusal makes no generation call. *(REQ-R2–REQ-R4, REQ-R6)*
- [ ] Answers use only retrieved passages; the prompt is versioned and each answer records its model, prompt version and latency; disabled or unavailable generation returns passages with a degraded outcome and no composed answer. *(REQ-G1–REQ-G6)*
- [ ] API responses validate against the answered, refused and degraded contracts; the browser shows citations, distinct answer/refusal/degraded/error states, pending state, phone-width layout and only working top-bar actions. *(REQ-A1, REQ-A2, REQ-A5, REQ-C1, REQ-C2, REQ-U1, REQ-U1b, REQ-U2–REQ-U7, REQ-U9–REQ-U11)*
- [ ] A follow-up uses recent configured conversation context, retrieval runs again using the conversation's user questions, and Reset starts a new conversation without earlier context. *(REQ-A3, REQ-C3–REQ-C7, REQ-U8)*
- [ ] The corpus browser lists all five documents with metadata and status, renders navigable sections as Markdown, opens citations at their named sections and offers routes back to the list and assistant. *(REQ-A4, REQ-B1–REQ-B6, REQ-U12, REQ-U15)*
- [ ] History is newest first, excludes conversations with no turns, and lets a user reopen a conversation and continue it with a follow-up. *(REQ-A6, REQ-C8, REQ-C9, REQ-U13, REQ-U14)*
- [ ] NFR-1–NFR-6 meet the PRD targets: answer p50 under 5s, refusal under 1.5s, service remains available in degraded mode, full ingestion under 60s, `/healthz` responds while Postgres or Qdrant is down, and secrets do not appear in logs, responses or commits. *(NFR-1–NFR-6)*
- [ ] One browser walkthrough completes a supported cited answer, an unsupported refusal, a contextual follow-up, citation navigation into the corpus and reopening/continuing from history; ingestion and evaluation command output is recorded separately. *(REQ-A1, REQ-C3, REQ-U5, REQ-U13, REQ-U15)*

*From the success measures in `docs/project-brief.md`.*

- S2 — more than 95% of answers carry a citation to a current policy version.
- S5 — more than 90% of out-of-scope questions are correctly declined rather than guessed at.
